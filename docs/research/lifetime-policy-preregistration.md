# Lifetime policy experiment specification

2026-09-30, recorded before the new vectorized lifecycle runs and policy fit.
This advances P05/P06 and then P04. The earlier measurements remain historical;
they cannot establish a gain against the stronger vectorized owner control.

## Objective and controls

The primary CPU objective is the complete serial stage sum from owner
representation discovery/certification through key generation, index and
unused-pool enrollment, every update, full checker preparation, native server
preparation and all adaptive online queries. No network throughput is assumed.
All originally prepared tokens are charged, even if they become unusable.
Memory/body models and transmitted bytes remain separate axes. This is a local
volatile experiment; authenticated durable migration is not implemented here.

First compare sparse encrypted repair with full fresh reencryption using the
same frozen maps, IDs, parameters, unused pads and edit/query trace. Both arms
use owner-private int8 coordinates and exact batched int64 products. Rebuilding
that coordinate cache is charged to both arms. Encryption remains fresh and
includes zero patches. The fallback Python arithmetic is retained.

Use rotating paired order and two repeats for the first screening runs:
rank 32 / d128 / m4096 / N2048 / t1153 / Q40, and the previous rank128 / d512 /
m16384 / N16384 / t1153 / Q40 workload. Pool sizes 16 and 32, respectively;
four and eight edits, respectively; two queries per nonfinal update and every
remaining token on the final revision. Inspect a dense-edit control if sparse
repair survives. These are synthetic crossover screens, not held-out Gate C.

Every accepted query must pass the complete full-vector check before secret
decryption and agree with all exact scores/stable top-3 IDs. Independently
compare every ciphertext coefficient with GMP and unreduced integer phases
once per revision, outside the measured stage sums. No private pad/query or
index-derived map is sent to a remote evaluator for preparation.

## Policy training and evaluation

Before fitting a policy, retain measured stage prices, source/input hashes and
raw repetitions. A calibrated predictor must report its held-out error; count
models alone do not become milliseconds. A bounded online policy may inspect
the current owner-approved edit, exact map feasibility, pending count, prior
edit statistics and public phase bound. It may not inspect future revisions,
future private queries or mask values. Hindsight E52 remains an oracle only.

Use disjoint trace seeds for training and evaluation, and preserve negative
shifted/adversarial traces. Compare static raw, static affine/no reserve,
owner-specified reserve, greedy repair/refresh, strongest ordinary delta
indexing, full reencryption and hindsight. Charge fitting/catalog exploration,
unused-token replacement, verification and migration. Do not select the best
held-out policy after seeing its outcomes. If a mechanism loses after a
stronger control, record the loss and change the research hypothesis.

For public data, separate schema-valid row replacements from arbitrary bit
flips. The latter may violate the source domain and must not be described as
observed application updates. A synthetic mutable-direction workload is an
explicit stress scenario, not evidence of customer workload frequency.

## Decision rules

The existing gates remain: useful outsourcing relative to permitted caches;
an algorithm beyond known affine/layout/incremental ingredients; at least 20%
complete amortized cost or 2x a binding resource on justified held-out
workloads; and a defensible transcript/security argument. These screens do
not by themselves meet those gates or justify a new GPU/service implementation.
