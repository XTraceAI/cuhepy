## Attema--Cramer--Xing, Mathematical Cryptology1(1),45--70 (2021)

Inspected PDFpp6--7 (printed50--51), Theorem2 and full proof; pp16--17
(printed60--61), complete Theorem8--10 statements/proofs. Theorem2 requires
L=Q(zeta_m), z|m, rad(z)=rad(m), p=1 modz and
ord_m(p)=phi(m)/phi(z). Its sufficient invertibility conditions are a small
nonzero coefficient2-norm or infinity-norm, compared to p^{1/phi(z)} with
embedding singular-value factors. The proof bounds the algebraic norm and
uses prime-ideal norms; its projection refinement uses the decomposition
field. These are deterministic conditions, not a raw-secret probability law.

The advertised optimality is precise: for prime-power conductor m, integral
bases with sufficiently small largest embedding singular value must contain
roots of unity, and such root-of-unity bases attain the same optimum. For
power-of-two conductors these bases are signed power-basis choices. This is
optimality of a basis/norm control, not of arbitrary modular root sets or
ternary annihilation mass. Inference: direct known containment of the norm
ingredient; no implication of structured-packet probability extremality.
Changing the sampler's basis outside signed permutations changes its law.

## Lyubashevsky--Nguyen--Seiler, retained ePrint2020/1183

Inspected complete PDFp7, Sections2.2--2.3 and Lemma2.1. The stated ring
factorization has l irreducible degree-d/l binomials, a primitive2l-th root,
and q-1=2l mod4l. Its challenge coefficients are IID with P(0)=1/2 and
P(+1)=P(-1)=1/4, unlike our raw uniform-ternary P(0)=1/3.

Lemma2.1 cites ALS2020 and bounds ONE coefficient of a binomial-factor
remainder using the scalar Fourier product (1/2)+(1/2)cos(...). Its discussed
invertibility consequence depends on the factorization regime and sufficiently
small residue-collision bound. It does not state arbitrary four-root
independence or a root-set maximization theorem. The paper's reported numerical
scalar values are not transferred or reproduced here. Inference: the actual
law and scalar/binomial scope are strong comparator limitations; general
ALS formulas, with the correct p, are the relevant known control for us.
