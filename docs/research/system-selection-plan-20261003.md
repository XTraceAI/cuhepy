## Decision and finite stopping rule

The strongest practical foundation is the homemade CUDA/BGV evaluator. A
candidate paper system must safely accept its complete encrypted output and
improve a measured, fully priced operating point. The finite Fourier certificate
is a separate high-risk mathematical lead; it must not indefinitely block
system integration. A systems contribution may use established cryptography,
but a distinct architecture/tradeoff must survive equally informed controls.

Complete exactly the bounded first components below, then make **one explicit
construction/claim decision**. A known, dominated or unproved outcome closes
that scoped lead. Do not append a successor toy grid or reinterpret a stopped
recipe as a winning system. New expensive execution requires a useful surviving
claim, a stated resource contract and independent review. No conference outcome
is promised and no production/security gate is waived.

## Claim and evaluation checklist at selection

A selected claim must name the new algorithm/architecture/theorem, its closest
controls, the replaced expensive constraint and its complete measured effect.
Include owner setup, index/key state, preparation, all internal and client/server
traffic, verification, private finish, cold/returning clients, updates, lifetimes
and retries. Use the same outputs and stable IDs/ties. Stage-only speedups and
model-only bytes do not establish a system improvement.

Originality and usefulness remain **unestablished** at this plan's creation.
External proof libraries stay labeled controls; no imported HE implementation
replaces our homemade evaluator. Independent cryptographic/parameter review,
private side-channel assurance, actual attestation and durable lifecycle remain
required before production claims.
